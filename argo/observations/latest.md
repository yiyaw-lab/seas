1. Everyone is focused on making agents more capable. But the emerging bottleneck may be making them legible. Subagents, evaluation harnesses, and MCP all reduce uncertainty about what an agent is doing, whether it worked, and how it connects to the outside world.
2. Everyone talks about agent autonomy. But the infrastructure being built is increasingly about constraint and coordination. Subagents isolate context, evaluation harnesses constrain quality, and MCP standardizes interaction.
3. The field appears to be moving from intelligence creation to intelligence management. The frontier signals are less about making models smarter and more about organizing, measuring, and connecting intelligence that already exists.
4. Everyone treats subagents, evaluation systems, and MCP as separate categories. They may actually be parts of the same stack: organization, verification, and interoperability are becoming the operating system of agentic intelligence.
5. The most valuable AI companies of the next few years may not be those with the best models, but those with the best control systems around models. The signals suggest governance infrastructure is becoming a competitive advantage.
6. The frontier seems to be shifting from monolithic systems toward modular systems. MCP modularizes capabilities, subagents modularize cognition, and evaluation harnesses modularize judgment.
7. Everyone is asking how to make agents do more. The more interesting question may be how to make them cooperate. All three signals point toward coordination becoming a first-class frontier problem.
