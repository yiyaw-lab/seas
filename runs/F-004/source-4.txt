###### Abstract

Report issue for preceding element

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly serving as personal assistants, where users share complex and diverse preferences over extended interactions. However, assessing how well LLMs can follow these preferences in realistic, long-term situations remains underexplored. This work proposes RealPref, a benchmark for evaluating realistic preference-following in personalized user-LLM interactions. RealPref features 100 user profiles, 1300 personalized preferences, four types of preference expression (ranging from explicit to implicit), and long-horizon interaction histories. It includes three types of test questions (multiple-choice, true-or-false, and open-ended), with detailed rubrics for LLM-as-a-judge evaluation. Results indicate that LLM performance significantly drops as context length grows and preference expression becomes more implicit, and that generalizing user preference understanding to unseen scenarios poses further challenges. RealPref and these findings provide a foundation for future research to develop user-aware LLM assistants that better adapt to individual needs. The code is available at [github.com/GG14127/RealPref](https://github.com/GG14127/RealPref "").

## 1 Introduction

Report issue for preceding element

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable performance across a wide range of general-purpose tasks, such as mathematical reasoning (Wang et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib32 "")), question answering (Touvron et al., [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib30 ""); Achiam et al., [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib1 "")). However, beyond serving as general-purpose assistants that support users in everyday contexts, there is a growing need for LLM-based assistants to support personalized needs such as recommendations and decision-making. As users engage in long-term interactions with LLMs, they naturally disclose personal information, such as individual preferences, habits, and characteristics, through various forms of expression. In turn, users expect assistants to adapt to such signals and provide tailored responses to serve individual needs. Despite rising interest in LLMs’ personalization, most efforts focus on simplified scenarios:

1. 1.


Simple Context: Existing works typically feature short-term context with few dialogue turns, and content centered on a single preference or personalization instruction (Salemi et al., [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib28 ""); Kumar et al., [2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib13 ""); Zhao et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib35 "")) rather than a simulated user. This framing resembles instruction-following rather than genuine personalization emerging from long-term dialogue.

Report issue for preceding element

2. 2.


Straightforward Preferences Expression: While real-world preference expressions are often implicit and scattered (e.g., in emotional cues), most studies assume explicit, immediately accessible traits (Jiang et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib12 "")), neglecting the challenge of generalizing preference understanding to unseen scenarios.

Report issue for preceding element

3. 3.


Limited Evaluation Methodology: Prevalent classification tasks diverge from natural user-LLM interactions (Zhao et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib35 ""); Jiang et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib12 "")), while generation tasks, though realistic, lack reliable criteria to assess response quality (Zhao et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04191v1#bib.bib35 "")).

In light of this, we aim to answer: can LLMs effectively (1) capture user preferences from complex user expressions, (2) retain and follow user preferences across extended interaction contexts, and (3) generalize user preference understanding to unseen scenarios?

Report issue for preceding element

We propose a comprehensive evaluation benchmark, RealPref. Unlike existing benchmarks that focus on standalone preferences or short-term context, RealPref is designed to simulate the complexities of authentic, long-horizon user-LLM interactions. It constructs a dynamic testing environment featuring diverse user biographies and multi-session conversation histories, where preferences are revealed through varying levels of expression—ranging from explicit statements to implicit cues. This design enables rigorous assessment of an agent’s ability to maintain consistency and adapt to user needs over time.
